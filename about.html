About

I am a software engineer who ended up doing a lot of machine learning, and I like the part where the two meet: deciding what a system should predict, building the pipeline that feeds it, and proving that the number it reports is real.

I am finishing an MS in Computer Science at Columbia on the machine learning track, graduating in December 2026. Before that I studied Data Science at Hong Kong Baptist University, where my final-year project on predictive maintenance for vehicle chassis systems became a first-author paper at ACML 2025.

In summer 2026 I was a Software Engineer Intern at Microsoft on the OfficePLUS AI Assistant, working on a React/TypeScript streaming chat engine and on telemetry forensics across Azure data stores. Since June 2026 I have also been a Data Analyst at Columbia Business School, building entity-resolution pipelines in Python and DuckDB on the university's HPC grid.

What I care about

Problem formulation before model choice. In the ACML work the first real contribution was turning "predict failure" into three targets the maintenance records could actually support.

Evaluation that survives leakage. Vehicle-wise chronological validation, entity-disjoint splits, and fold-safe feature engineering are not glamorous, but they are the difference between a result and a number.

Explicit boundaries between what code guarantees and what a model suggests. Oracle OS puts every symbol in code and lets the model only explain; the OfficePLUS work was partly about proving which fallback branches were load-bearing before touching them.
