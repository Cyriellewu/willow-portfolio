ACML 2025 / PMLR 304 / First author

The model was not the first problem.

First-author research in a four-author collaboration. The work began with an industrial prediction request, then became a story about problem definition, usable labels, representation design, and evaluation that could survive leakage.

Contribution

I reframed an ambiguous prediction request into three testable maintenance decisions.

The initial fault and operational data did not line up with a usable outcome. After stakeholder clarification, I linked fault histories to maintenance records and defined three supervised tasks: emergency-fee existence, cost tier, and subsystem category.

Artifact

A weighted fault graph made relationships first-class.

I moved from sparse one-hot codes to contextual features and then to weighted graphs combining temporal, spatial, and repeated co-occurrence evidence. A GCN learned relational representations, and a Random Forest produced the task-level predictions.

Result

High-cost failure precision improved from about 60% to 95%.

The published evaluation uses vehicle-wise chronological cross-validation to reduce temporal and inter-vehicle leakage. The point was not just a strong score, but avoiding one that looked strong because future records or the same vehicles had leaked across the split.

Constraint

A usable target and leakage-resistant evaluation matter before model novelty.

More records are not automatically more evidence. The research used proprietary manufacturer datasets, so this case study keeps the published result and method while omitting dataset examples, internal figures, and unpublished implementation details.
