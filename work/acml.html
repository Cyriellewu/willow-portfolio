Predictive maintenance as cost-aware graph learning

A vehicle manufacturer asked for a model that predicts failures. Most of the work came before the model: deciding what the maintenance records could actually support as a target, turning fault relationships into a graph, and evaluating in a way that could not leak across vehicles or time.

Role
First author and lead researcher
Data
About 9M proprietary vehicle maintenance records
Method
Weighted fault graphs, GCN encoder, Random Forest head
Stack
Python, PyTorch Geometric, scikit-learn

Problem

The raw fault logs and operational readings did not line up with any usable outcome. "Predict failure" had no definition that the data could be scored against.

After clarifying with the stakeholders, I linked fault histories to maintenance records and defined three supervised tasks: whether an emergency fee occurred, which cost tier it fell in, and which subsystem was involved. Each one is a decision a maintenance planner actually makes.

Representation

Sparse one-hot fault codes carried almost no structure. I moved to contextual features, then to weighted graphs that combine three kinds of evidence between faults: temporal proximity, spatial proximity within the chassis, and repeated co-occurrence. A graph convolutional network learns relational representations over that graph, and a Random Forest produces the task-level predictions.

Result

The published evaluation uses vehicle-wise chronological cross-validation, so no vehicle and no future record appears on both sides of a split. The point was not only a strong score but a score that could not have come from leakage.

60% to 95%

Precision on the high-cost class in the emergency-fee range task, as reported in PMLR 304.

  • 98.9% fault-category accuracy.
  • High-cost failure precision from 60% to 95%, recall up 25 points.
  • Method: NRP-GCN-RF with triple-edge graph construction and vehicle-wise chronological cross-validation.
  • Read the paper in PMLR

Limits

The datasets are proprietary to the manufacturer. This page keeps the published results and method and omits dataset examples, internal figures, and unpublished implementation details.
