Palm-vein image enhancement

Dirt, warmth, and moisture degrade near-infrared palm-vein images. For a biometric, a smoother image is not automatically a better one: the enhancement has to preserve the structure a matcher depends on. This project measures that, and only that.

Role
Lead implementation and evaluation
Method
InstructPix2Pix with FFT low-frequency preservation and reference blending
Metrics
SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS against clean references
Conditions
Dirty, Warm, Wet

Method

A condition-specific InstructPix2Pix pipeline edits the degraded image, an FFT step preserves the low-frequency content of the reference, and the result is blended back toward the reference. The three degradation conditions are evaluated separately instead of being treated as interchangeable noise.

Degraded inputDirty, Warm, or Wet condition
Guided enhancementInstructPix2Pix with FFT preservation and blending
Similarity evaluationSSIM, PSNR, LPIPS against the clean reference

Results

Two separate readouts that are deliberately not merged, because they used different comparator setups.

Controlled 50-pair experimentMean SSIM by condition: Dirty 0.460, Warm 0.438, Wet 0.455. PSNR 21.68, 20.90, 19.80. LPIPS 0.517, 0.497, 0.505. A directional readout at n=50.
Archived 1,526-row QA checkMean before-and-after SSIM 0.939 across 1,526 comparison rows. Condition membership was not retained, so it stays separate from the controlled experiment.

Limits

The project produced comparison triplets, heatmaps, and histograms, but no biometric-derived imagery is published here pending data-license and consent review. The public record is the aggregate evaluation and the method description.
